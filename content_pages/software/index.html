MEDeA

For multilingual-translation architecture experiments

MEDeA (Multiway Encoder Decoder Architecture) is a fully parametrizable software for translation experiments on small datasets. Can also be used in historical linguistics for cognate prediction.

For a use case, see all my publications.

EtymDB 2

Etymological database

This fine grained etymological database is extracted from a 2019 Wiktionary dump. It differentiates between inheritance, cognacy, borrowing, and different types of composition.

It is provided with several scripts for data analysis, including one to draw your own language trees from the word links.

I contributed to the extraction of the 2019 dump, duplicate management in v2 of the base, and all the extraction scripts and notebooks present in the repo - the original Perl scripts were developed by B. Sagot

LEASP

For neurodegenerative diseases prediction

LEASP (Learning Spatiotemporal Patterns) is a C++ tool to estimate the group average and individual spatio-temporal trajectories of patients with neurodegenerative diseases.

Logic coded by I. Koval. I carried out the user interface, industrialisation (CI, coded the test suite), documentation, and dissemination (Docker packaging and work presentation to the Human Brain Project partners).

LEASP has since then been ported to Python/Pytorch, go check LEASPy out, it’s quite cool! Introductory article by I. Koval on Medium

PLexGen

For phonetic historical conlanging

If you want to try conlanging, you can with PLexGen, my phonetic lexicon generator and sound change applier! This little software is parametrizable, with json files to provide your language shape (phonetics and phonotactics) and text files to provide your sound changes.

It will then generate randomly a proto-language and its n daughter languages after your specifications.
